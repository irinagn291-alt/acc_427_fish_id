# Fish ID

## What it is

Fish ID helps anglers and fish observers identify fish from a photograph and keep a personal record of confirmed finds. It analyzes photos on the device, offers an illustrated field guide, and includes an offline reference for Florida freshwater fishing limits.

## Launch and onboarding

1. A cold launch opens the “Identify” tab directly. There is no onboarding, account setup, or permission prompt at launch.
2. The header reads “Fish ID” and has a sliders icon with the accessibility label “Preferences”. The opening panel reads “ON-DEVICE • OFFLINE” and “Meet your  
   next discovery.” It shows a fish drawing captioned “Field illustration” and “Perca fluviatilis”.
3. “Open camera” and “Choose a photo” start identification. Below them, “One fish. A clear side view.” is followed by “Fill the frame with the fish. Suggestions are a starting point: compare the markings before you confirm.” “Try a sample photo” opens a built-in example.
4. The “SPECIES OF THE DAY” card shows a rotating illustrated species with its common and scientific names. “22 illustrated species” opens the guide. The tab bar shows “Identify”, “Field guide”, “My finds”, and “Limits”.

## Screens

### “Identify”

- “Preferences” is the sliders icon. It opens the “Preferences” sheet.
- “Open camera” opens the native still camera when available and authorized. Accepting the captured photo opens “Identify” with the image. Cancelling returns to the home screen.
- “Choose a photo” opens the native Photos picker for still images. Choosing an image opens “Identify” with that photo. Dismissing the picker returns to the home screen.
- “Try a sample photo” opens the same identification flow with a bundled rainbow trout photo.
- Tapping the “SPECIES OF THE DAY” card opens that species’ profile. Tapping “22 illustrated species” switches to “Field guide”.

### Photo results — “Identify”

- The selected photo appears above “Reading the details…” while analysis runs. The sample adds “Sample photo · Sam Stukel / USFWS” and “Sample scans are not added to your statistics.”
- “Possible matches” lists up to three suggestions, each with its common name, scientific name, percentage, and selection indicator. When matches are available, the caption reads “Model score, not certainty”. Tap a row to choose it. The selection reveals “Look for these details” and a link labeled with the species’ common name, which opens its profile.
- “Choose species manually” opens a sheet with the same title and all 22 species listed by common and scientific name. Tap a row to select it and return to the photo. “Cancel” on this sheet returns to the photo with the previous selection.
- “Field notes” accepts an optional multiline note. The keyboard’s “Done” control dismisses the keyboard.
- “Confirm & save find” saves the photo, chosen species, date, and note, closes the results, and switches to “My finds”. The sample uses “Done” in place of the save button and returns to the home screen. The results toolbar’s “Cancel” closes the photo without saving a find.

### “Field guide”

- “22 illustrated species” appears above a grid of fish cards. Each card shows an illustration, common name, scientific name, typical adult length, and a heart if favorited. Tap a card to open its species profile.
- “Name or scientific name” searches either name as you type. “Favorites” toggles the grid between all species and favorited species and can be combined with search. In UK English, the toggle reads “Favourites”.

### Species profile — titled with the species’ common name

- Shows a fish illustration, common and scientific names, “Look for these details”, “Typical adult length”, “Habitat & behavior”, and “Native range” where provided. UK English uses “Habitat & behaviour”.
- The heart icon toggles the species as a favorite. Its accessibility label is “Favorite species” or “Favourite species” in UK English.
- Seasonal profiles show “Spawning • Northern Hemisphere” and “Timing varies with latitude and water temperature. This is biology, not an open fishing season.” Other profiles use “Breeding”, with text such as “Young disperse as planktonic larvae” or “Live young in warm months”.
- “Photo identification” displays either “Included in photo suggestions” or “Field guide and manual selection”. The five manual-only profiles are “Florida bass”, “European perch”, “Tench”, “Walleye”, and “Black crappie”.
- “Compare species” opens the comparison screen. “Limits” opens the Florida reference. The illustration credit reads “Field illustration • Fish ID”. When a widget opens the profile in a sheet, “Done” closes it and leaves “Field guide” selected.

### “Compare species”

- “Compare with” opens a menu of the other guide species, labeled by common name. A comparison species is selected when the screen opens. Choosing another updates the second card while keeping the original species.
- Both cards show illustrations, common and scientific names, identifying marks, typical adult length, and habitat. They appear side by side when space permits and stack on narrower layouts. The navigation back control returns to the original profile.

### “My finds”

- “Scans this week” counts completed analyses of personal photos in the current calendar week, including analyses that return no reliable match. “Species collected” counts distinct species among saved finds.
- “Seasonal photo challenge” shows progress from “0/3” to “3/3” and reads “Photograph and confirm 3 different species this season. Observe gently and release fish responsibly.”
- An empty notebook shows “Your field notebook starts here.” and “Identify a photo and confirm the species to keep your first find.” The “Identify” button switches to that tab.
- Saved finds appear newest first. Each row shows a photo thumbnail or species illustration, common name, date and time, and the first line of the note. Tap a row to open its detail.

### Find detail — “My finds”

- Shows the saved photo, scientific name, and date and time. “Field notes” edits the note. “Save note” saves the edit and keeps the detail open. The link labeled with the common name opens the species profile.
- “Save find card to Photos” creates a card headed “Fish ID” with the photo, common and scientific names, date, and current note text, including edits still in the field. Success shows “Card saved to Photos”.
- “Delete find” opens “Delete this photo and field note from this device?” Confirm with “Delete find” to remove the entry and local photo and return to the list. “Cancel” dismisses the confirmation.

### “Limits”

- Shows “Jurisdiction”, “Offline reference • Florida, USA”, and “Checked 27 Nov 2026”.
- The warning reads “Statewide freshwater summary. Local waters, permits and protected species can have stricter rules. Verify official rules before keeping any fish. Photo recognition does not establish legal identity.”
- The five numbered entries read:

  1. “Black bass: 5 per day in total; only one may be 16 in (40.6 cm) or longer. Florida/largemouth bass: no statewide minimum length. Other black bass species have additional restrictions.”
  2. “American eel: 25 per angler per day; minimum total length 9 in (22.9 cm). Bait purchase exemptions apply.”
  3. “Butterfly peacock bass: 2 per day; only one may be 17 in (43.2 cm) or longer.”
  4. “Alligator gar and grass carp: possession requires a permit; release immediately without one.”
  5. “Panfish have a combined daily limit of 50. The daily limit for crappie is 25. Local waters may have different bag and length limits.”

- Under “Official source”, “Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission ↗” and “Florida Wildlife Code · 68A-23.005 ↗” open external references. The section ends with “Other regions are not covered by this offline edition.”

### “Preferences”

- “Local reminders” reads “Get a species reminder at 9:00 and a photo challenge on the first day of each season. Scheduled on this device; no server is involved.”
- “Enable reminders” requests notification permission and, if granted, schedules the daily 9:00 reminder and reminders at 9:00 on March 1, June 1, September 1, and December 1. “Turn off reminders” cancels pending reminders. “Open Settings” opens the app’s system settings.
- The daily notification uses “SPECIES OF THE DAY” and “Meet today’s species in your offline field guide.” The seasonal notification uses “Seasonal photo challenge” and “Photograph and confirm 3 different species this season. Observe gently and release fish responsibly.”
- “Privacy & model” says “Photos and notes stay on this device. No account, tracking, advertising or uploads. Deleting a find removes its local photo. Backups follow your device settings. Widgets read only a local summary.”
- The identification explanation reads “iNaturalist runs on this device. Photo suggestions cover 17 fish species. The illustrated guide has 22 profiles. Scores express the model’s ranking. Check the visible marks and record a species only when you can confirm it.”
- The credits read “iNaturalist model and taxonomy · MIT licence. Sample photograph by Sam Stukel, U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service · Public domain.” US English uses “MIT license”. “iNaturalist ↗” opens the attribution reference and “Sample photograph · USFWS ↗” opens the photo credit.
- The footer reads “Fish ID · 1.3.0 (2037)”. “Done” closes the sheet.

### “Fish ID field notes” widget

- The system widget gallery describes it as “A daily species, your collection and spawning notes.”
- Small Home Screen and Lock Screen formats show the daily species. The large Home Screen format adds “Favorites”, “Recent finds”, a total find count, and the daily species’ spawning or breeding information. Those formats open the daily species profile when tapped.
- The medium Home Screen format shows “Fish ID”, “Weekly field notes”, “Scans this week”, and a seven-day bar chart. Tapping it opens “My finds”.

- “Open camera” and “Choose a photo” for offline fish photo identification across 17 species.
- “Possible matches” with model scores, identifying details, and “Choose species manually”.
- “Try a sample photo” for a guided example using the normal identification flow.
- “Field guide” with 22 illustrated species, common and scientific name search, “Favorites”, and “Compare species”.
- “SPECIES OF THE DAY” with a rotating species profile.
- “My finds” with confirmed photos, “Field notes”, “Save note”, “Save find card to Photos”, and “Delete find”.
- “Scans this week”, “Species collected”, and “Seasonal photo challenge”.
- “Limits” with an offline Florida freshwater reference and official source links.
- “Local reminders” for the daily species and the seasonal photo challenge.
- “Fish ID field notes” Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets.

## Behaviours that can look like bugs

- “Confirm & save find” is disabled until a species is selected. Tap a row under “Possible matches” or use “Choose species manually”. “Field notes” is optional.
- “No reliable fish match. Try a closer side-on photo or choose a species from the atlas.” means the photo returned no suggestions. For a low-scoring result, the message is “No confident match. Try a closer, well-lit side view or select a species from the guide.” Use “Choose species manually” or tap “Cancel” and try another photo.
- “The local model could not finish. Try another photo; the field guide is still available.” appears when analysis fails. Manual selection is still available. “This image could not be read. Choose another photo.” means the image could not be opened. Retry through “Choose a photo”.
- “Try a sample photo” returns to “Identify” when you tap “Done”. The sample creates no find and adds no scan to statistics. Use “Open camera” or “Choose a photo” to save a personal find.
- “Scans this week” can increase without a new entry in “My finds”. A completed analysis of a personal photo counts even if it returns no match or you exit without confirming. The scan count starts over with the calendar week. Saving the same species again adds another find but leaves “Species collected” unchanged.
- “Seasonal photo challenge” counts distinct confirmed species saved during the current season. Another find of the same species leaves the challenge count unchanged. Progress starts over at the next season, and deleting a find can reduce progress if it was the only record of that species for the season.
- “Favorite a species to keep it close.” appears when the filtered “Favorites” view is empty, including when a search excludes all favorite species. In UK English, it reads “Favourite a species to keep it close.” Clear the search, turn off “Favorites” or “Favourites”, or add a species with the profile heart. “No species found” appears on an empty search without the favorites filter.
- “Your field notebook starts here.” remains until the first personal find is confirmed. Tap “Identify”, choose or take a fish photo, select a species, and tap “Confirm & save find”.
- “Camera access is unavailable. Choose a photo instead, or enable the camera in Settings.” covers denied access and unavailable camera hardware. Use “Choose a photo” or enable access in system settings.
- “Notifications are disabled. You can enable them in system Settings.” appears when notifications are denied. Use “Open Settings”, allow notifications, then return to “Preferences” and tap “Enable reminders”. “Allow adding photos in Settings to export a find card.” means Photos add access is required before retrying the export.
- “The find could not be saved. Free some device storage and try again.” is the local storage error. Free space and retry. App alerts are headed “Fish ID” and have an “OK” dismissal button.

## Starter content and resume

“Try a sample photo” provides a rainbow trout photograph credited to Sam Stukel / USFWS. The 22 illustrated profiles, rotating “SPECIES OF THE DAY”, and Florida reference are available on first launch. “My finds” and “Favorites” start empty, and reminders start off.

Saved finds, saved notes, favorites, scan history, and the reminder preference persist between launches. An unconfirmed photo, species selection, or unsaved note edit has no draft to resume after closing the flow or relaunching.

## Permissions

- **Camera.** Requested when “Open camera” is tapped for the first time on a device with an available camera. The English usage description is “Photograph a fish for private, on-device identification.”
- **Photos, add only.** Requested when “Save find card to Photos” needs access to add a card. The English usage description is “Add an illustrated card of your confirmed find to Photos.”
- **Photos picker.** “Choose a photo” opens the system picker and grants access to the selected image without asking for broad library access. The configured English library usage description is “Choose a fish image to analyze locally in Fish ID.”
- **Notifications.** Requested when the user taps “Enable reminders”. iOS supplies the prompt wording. There is no app-specific usage-description string.

## Absent

Login and accounts, in-app purchases, ads, analytics, an account deletion flow, and an App Tracking Transparency prompt are absent. Users create private content through photos and “Field notes”. There is no public posting or community feed.

## Data and support

Photo analysis, saved photos, notes, favorites, scan history, and reminders stay on the device. Widgets read a local summary. Device backups follow system settings. A card exported to Photos follows the user’s Photos settings and can remain in Photos after the original find is deleted from Fish ID.

There is no in-app “Contact” or “Support” control. “Preferences” provides “Open Settings” and attribution links. “Limits” provides official reference links.

## Scanning and health

None. The app analyzes still photos of fish and expects a clear side view. It has no barcode or QR scanner and displays no health, medical, or product-health information.

## Platform

iPhone and iPad, with a minimum of iOS 17.4. iPhone supports portrait and both landscape orientations. iPad also supports upside-down portrait. The app uses a light appearance.

The field guide and photo identification work offline across regions. “Limits” always covers Florida, USA, with no region selector or location permission. Daily and seasonal features use the device calendar. The project includes English, US English, UK English, German, French, Spanish, Arabic, and Traditional Chinese localizations. The quoted copy above uses English, with relevant English variants called out.
